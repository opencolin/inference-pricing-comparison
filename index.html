Inference pricing analysis

Baseten vs Nebius Token Factory for DeepSeek V4 Flash

The list prices sit within 8% of each other. Prompt caching, not the rate card, decides which one is cheaper — and the gap it opens reaches 74%.

Published 12 August 2026 · Prices verified against vendor sources on that date

The short version. Baseten lists DeepSeek V4 Flash at $0.13/$0.26 per 1M input/output tokens; Nebius Token Factory lists $0.14/$0.28. That 7.7% spread is noise. The decisive difference is that Baseten prices cached input at $0.028/1M — a 78% discount — while Nebius has no billable prompt caching at all.

On a workload with no prefix reuse the two are interchangeable on cost. On an agentic workload with 90% cache hits, Baseten costs 42% less.

Nebius is uniformly 7.7% above Baseten on both directions, so the input/output mix of a workload changes absolute spend but not the ratio between the two providers. Nebius serves the model from us-central1; both quote roughly comparable throughput, discussed below.

Prompt caching is the whole story

Baseten's cached-input rate of $0.028/1M is 78% off its own list input price. Nebius does not currently bill cached input at a discount — an open feature request from March 2026 asks for exactly this, observing that the platform is at a disadvantage in repeated, input-heavy scenarios.

This matters disproportionately for DeepSeek V4 Flash specifically, because the model is positioned for coding and agentic workloads. Agentic loops are the maximal caching case: every tool call re-sends the accumulated conversation as a shared prefix, so input cache hit rates of 70–90% are typical rather than optimistic.

Volume discounts are unpublished

Both vendors offer volume discounts and neither publishes the numbers. Nebius promises "volume discounts as you scale"; Baseten offers them across all tiers. No threshold or percentage appears in either vendor's own documentation.

Third-party aggregators circulate a specific Nebius ladder — 10% off at 1M tokens/month rising to 50% at 1B — attributed to Nebius pricing but with no verifiable citation, and those figures appear nowhere on Nebius's own site. This analysis does not rely on them, and neither should a procurement decision.

What the verified list prices do support: Nebius would need roughly a 7% discount to match Baseten at zero cache reuse, and roughly 42% to match it at 90% cache hits. Whether either vendor offers that is a question for a quote, not a rate card — anyone operating at this volume should price both against their own traffic profile.

Baseten's self-serve tiers cap at 120 RPM regardless of plan, which is roughly an eighth of the reference workload's request rate — reaching it requires an Enterprise agreement. Nebius raises limits dynamically, growing 20% per 15-minute window while utilisation stays above 80%, up to 20× the base allocation before an Enterprise plan is needed.

The practical consequence is that Nebius can be scaled into without a sales cycle, while Baseten cannot. For teams that need to be in production before a contract closes, that operational difference may outweigh a double-digit cost gap.

Throughput

Nebius's own console advertises 291.77 tok/s for this model. Artificial Analysis, measuring independently, puts Nebius at 252 tok/s and Baseten at 273 tok/s. A vendor self-report and a third-party benchmark are not directly comparable, and neither reflects the prompt shapes of any given application — treat throughput as a wash pending a first-party test.

Method and caveats

  • Costs are computed from published per-token rates at the stated reference workload; they exclude volume discounts, committed-use pricing, and batch rates.
  • A 30-day month at continuous full utilisation is assumed. Real traffic is bursty, so these figures are ceilings for a workload with that peak rate.
  • The 70/30 input/output split is illustrative. Because Nebius's premium is identical on both directions, changing the split moves both providers proportionally.
  • Cache hit rate refers to input tokens served from a matched prefix. Output tokens never cache and bill at full rate for both providers.
  • Nebius pricing was read directly from the Token Factory console. Third-party aggregators carried a materially different figure for this model — more than 2× off — and separately circulate volume-discount tiers that Nebius itself does not publish. Verify any rate against the vendor's own console before relying on it.
  • Dedicated GPU hosting was considered and excluded: at this throughput, sustaining the decode load would take substantially more accelerator capacity than the equivalent per-token spend buys at either vendor's published GPU rates.
